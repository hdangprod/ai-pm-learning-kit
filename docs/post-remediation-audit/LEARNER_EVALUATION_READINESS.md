# Learner evaluation readiness

**Current status: CONDITIONAL GO.** This is a learner-safe plan; it contains no form answers or instructor keys. It describes a first-person study with one learner and independent reviewers. No study has been run.

## Purpose

Observe whether you can calculate, explain and make a bounded product decision on unfamiliar tasks after guided learning, and whether the reasoning remains available about seven days later. Separate this from learning to complete the app's fixed practice exercises.

The study can provide a useful personal competency profile and feasibility evidence. With one learner, different forms and no randomized control, it cannot establish that the app caused any improvement or works better than a textbook.

## Conditions before baseline

An independent moderator/reviewer should confirm all of these:

1. The study uses a pinned app release that works on the actual computer/browser, including the export, retry and review workflows. This audit did not complete the real-browser suite.
2. First responses have a dependable external capture procedure, or the identified storage-preservation weaknesses have been fixed and tested. Use one app window/profile; do not edit from another tab/device sharing its storage. Stop if a storage warning appears.
3. The assigned forms and keys have been reviewed for wording, scoring alternatives, prior exposure, prerequisites and time. Do not assume 45 minutes is enough.
4. You have not read the assigned tasks, keys or detailed reviewer audit. If you have seen them, the moderator needs fresh reviewed reserve forms. Having a file available is not proof that you read it, but lack of exposure must be checked rather than assumed.
5. Two reviewers have calibrated on separate authored examples. They know how to score a correct but unusual decision and a justified decision to keep the same direction.
6. The moderator has a session log, sealed forms, change cards, an external archive and a final independence/adjudication checklist. A creation-time “eligible” flag is not the final independence decision.

Moderator dry runs may happen now. Baseline collection starts only after these conditions are signed off. The learner should receive only this plan and the assigned learner directory, not the complete repository, form container or keys.

## People and roles

- **Learner:** complete your own responses and report help/exposure honestly.
- **Moderator:** assign and release one form, control the change card, record time/help and preserve artifacts. The moderator may be one of the reviewers if this limitation is disclosed.
- **Two reviewers:** score anonymized calculations, explanations and decision memos independently before discussion. Withhold app points, model answers, timepoint and assistance records during the first scoring pass where feasible.

If only one reviewer is available, the study can still be a preliminary self-study exercise, but it does not meet this plan's independently reviewed scoring condition. Do not invent a second score or call AI output a human review.

## Baseline, immediate and delayed assessment

The moderator assigns different reviewed forms or reserve forms at the three timepoints. A single learner's form sequence does not remove form-difficulty differences. Do not compare raw totals as if the instruments were equated.

During each independent assessment:

- Calculator or an empty spreadsheet is allowed. No app, AI, internet, notes, hints or keys.
- Use Vietnamese or English consistently. The reviewer scores reasoning, not English style.
- Show your calculation and explain the unit, population, time window, permitted conclusion and useful next check. A valid statement that data are insufficient can be a good answer when justified.
- Preserve the original decision memo before receiving changed information. The moderator releases only that form's card; write the revision separately.
- At the time limit, submit the work as it stands. Record unfinished tasks and why they were unfinished.

The moderator records actual start/end times, breaks, help, interruptions, outside tools and exposure. The CLI provides files and fields but does not automatically observe or certify these facts. Any help changes the interpretation of that response; it does not justify deleting the response.

Do not release keys or repair your baseline/post response before delayed testing. Ordinary work and English study may continue; record related study, AI use or task exposure during the interval. Delayed performance after extra practice must not be attributed solely to the lab.

## Guided learning and first-response evidence

Use the six existing modules. Start with your hypothesis, explore the data, make your first response, then inspect feedback. If you retry, write what changed in your reasoning. You may use instructor guidance during this learning phase; record it.

After each first submitted practice response, preserve an external copy of the export or a moderator observation record before continuing. Keep later retries separately. The external archive is essential if the evaluation uses app history to distinguish initial, feedback-assisted and solution-assisted performance. Do not reset activities during a study round.

If a storage warning appears, stop editing, preserve the raw record and contact the moderator. Continuing to type into a recovered empty session can replace older work. If a second window is accidentally opened, close the stale window without saving and record the incident. Rehearse this procedure in the dry run.

“Initial unaided” inside the app means no recorded in-app assistance for that context. It does not verify absence of help elsewhere. A same-case transfer response after the lesson is still guided practice, not your independent assessment result.

## Independent review

Reviewers first score original anonymous responses. They record evidence for each dimension, critical claims and disagreements, then adjudicate without deleting initial ratings. Oral follow-up questions and repaired work are scored or described separately.

Report three different kinds of evidence:

| Evidence | What it can show |
|---|---|
| App practice and retry history | Interaction with feedback and changes during learning |
| Independent written calculations/explanations/memos | Performance on the assigned task under the recorded conditions |
| Delayed response and neutral explanation | Persistence and application to that later task, subject to prior exposure and form differences |

A high structured score does not replace a reviewed memo. A low score under an unvalidated time limit may reflect reading/time difficulty as well as understanding. Review the error pattern, not just the total.

## Interpretation and decision rules

- **Practice effect:** improvement on the familiar fixture or after repeated hints may show practice with that task.
- **Exposure effect:** remembering an answer, a previously seen form or a worked example weakens an unseen interpretation.
- **Transfer evidence:** solving an unfamiliar task, explaining the rule and its limits, and responding coherently to a changed condition is stronger evidence of application. A delayed repetition strengthens the observation but does not establish a causal app effect.

One learner, non-equated forms, different instruction-to-test intervals, reviewer judgment and outside study all limit conclusions. Use a profile by competency and error type; do not claim a gain percentage, general educational effectiveness or professional AI PM readiness.

If reliable first-attempt records are missing, mark that part unverified. If a form was exposed, call the response exposed; do not silently relabel it independent. If no delayed session occurs, the delayed outcome is unknown, not zero ability. If reviewer disagreements remain unresolved, report both interpretations and do not award independent success by averaging away a critical error.

The next learning decision should be specific: revisit the concept behind a recurring error, simplify an unclear instruction, or run another fresh task with a reviewer. Completing all six app modules is not the study's success criterion.
